Case Study · Current Project

Modernization of the Milla River Early Warning System (EWS)

Environmental, geospatial, and hydrometeorological characterization supporting community-based risk management for flash floods in Mérida, Venezuela.

Back to GIS Profile
Project Context

Expanding an Existing Operational Foundation

Unidad de Respuesta Operativa (URO) is a risk management NGO that has developed a community-based flood monitoring and communication system for the Milla River watershed, located in a mountainous metropolitan area of the Venezuelan Andes. Its current operation combines stream-stage observations from calibrated staff gauges, community rain gauges, historical records, GIS products, and direct reports from residents and operational personnel.

This local capacity is the project's starting point. The modernization effort is intended to organize and connect the existing components, reduce manual tasks, and support more timely interpretation of watershed conditions.

Objective

Toward an Integrated, Semi-Automated Early Warning System

The proposed system will centralize field observations and weather station data while progressively incorporating sensors, satellite meteorological products, and community reports into a semi-automated workflow. Maps, indicators, and operational dashboards will support event assessment and warning activation.

The system is designed to support URO personnel and the community in monitoring and decision-making, strengthening preparedness for flash floods in the context of climate change and the watershed's high geomorphological and hydrological sensitivity.

Work Completed

Technical Design and Development of the Initial Components

My work includes designing the modernization proposal and developing the initial hydrometeorological and geospatial components of the system.

System Design

Definition of a modular architecture integrating data sources, automation, processing, database and cloud storage, visualization, and warning functions while accounting for URO's technical resources and operational requirements.

Hydrometeorological Component

Automation of data acquisition and consolidation from local weather stations and satellite products, followed by time-series analysis to support statistical modeling and the future definition of warning thresholds for key variables.

Geospatial Component

Biophysical, geomorphological, and hydrological characterization of the watershed; collection and structuring of territorial data; and development of maps and indicators for environmental and risk analysis.

Data and Methodology

Integration of Local, Territorial, and Satellite Data

The work combines field observations, meteorological records, historical information, and geospatial data to develop a more comprehensive understanding of watershed conditions.

Data Sources

  • Local monitoring: automated weather stations, community rain gauges, and staff gauges.
  • Historical records: documented flood events and incident reports from URO and the community.
  • Terrain and hydrology: derivatives from ALOS PALSAR and Copernicus DEM.
  • Land cover and physical environment: Sentinel-2, Dynamic World, SoilGrids, USGS, and INGEOMIN data.
  • Basemap and reference data: OpenStreetMap, GADM, and institutional geospatial sources.
  • Hydrometeorological context: Weathercloud station data, GPM IMERG, ERA5-Land, and CHIRPS.

Current Results

Technical Progress and Deliverables

Work completed to date includes development of the hydrometeorological and geospatial components, along with the technical design of subsequent implementation phases.

01

Hydrometeorological Component

Development of semi-automated workflows to ingest, consolidate, and update data from two weather stations, combined with exploratory and comparative analysis of time series, data continuity, and precipitation events.

02

Geospatial Data Preparation

Collection, evaluation, cleaning, and standardization of data on terrain and drainage, the physical environment and land cover, infrastructure, exposure, and other territorial constraints.

03

Watershed and Morphometric Analysis

Delineation and characterization of the watershed and its internal units using terrain variables, the drainage network, and morphometric indicators to interpret hydrological response and support susceptibility and risk assessment.

04

System Design and Technical Documentation

Development of reports, maps, and indicators, together with a comprehensive modernization proposal defining the architecture, system components, data flows, and phased implementation plan for the Early Warning System.

Next Steps for the EWS

The next phases will focus on transforming the developed components into an integrated operational workflow, aligned with available data, equipment, and resources.

1

Operational Integration

  • Centralization and standardization of historical time series.
  • Automated data ingestion and quality control.
  • Visualization of data, sensors, events, and mapping products.
  • Generation of semi-automated warnings for the URO team.
2

Expansion and Refinement

  • Gradual integration of additional data sources and monitoring locations.
  • Updates to territorial and risk zoning.
  • Warning-threshold calibration using documented events.
  • Subsequent development of HEC-HMS hydrologic and HEC-RAS hydraulic models.
